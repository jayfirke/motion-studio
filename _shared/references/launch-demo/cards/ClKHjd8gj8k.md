## Visual style
Two worlds: indigo with live action in skewed glass panels for people, and white-to-lavender haze for product UI. One electric violet accent and a clean geometric sans. Depth from tilted screens, soft shadows and translucent panels.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
Real product screens, never full-frame: always tilted in 3D and slowly travelling.
- A violet arrow cursor does the work: picks a date (0:32), clicks "Accept" (0:47), and sits on the CTA button (1:12).
- Close-up punch-ins show the exact control that matters: a ticked checkbox, an apostille button (0:35).
- Hybrid shots composite UI onto live action, either a screen replaced in a hand-held phone (0:43) or a UI card floating beside a person at a laptop (0:47), so the person and the click share one frame.
- Waiting is shown with a dial (1:00), not by cutting it out.

## Motion and transitions
Parallelogram mask wipes slide footage across the indigo frame (0:00–0:10). The logo builds from two dots (0:10.7–0:14) and one dot returns for the end card (1:09), a bookend carry. In the UI section the camera glides across tilted screens with long, soft eases, and popovers scale out of their trigger.

## Pacing
Measured: 111 words/min, a median hold of 5.4 s and 13 cuts. The script follows the user journey in strict order, one step per sentence and one screen per step, so it never feels rushed or lost. The two breaths come at the logo build (0:11–0:16) and the processing dial.

## Sound

## What to take from it
- 0:29–0:39: a real UI tilt plus a cursor that picks a date, then a punch-in on the one ticked checkbox. This is demo grammar that explains a form in 10 s.
- 0:47: a person and a UI card in the same frame, where the cursor clicks "Accept" and the card reacts. The cause is visible.
- 1:00–1:03: a progress dial, then a "ready" banner with a Download button, which compresses waiting into 3 s.
- 0:10.7–0:14 and 1:09: a dot → logo bookend.

## Avoid
- Stock business footage and smiling-at-laptop casting.
- Parallelogram glass panels (dated corporate look).
- The low-poly map and the avatar globe.
- A −20 LUFS mix.
