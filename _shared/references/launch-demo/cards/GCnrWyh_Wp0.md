## Visual style
Two halves that do not match. The hook is a dark cinematic look: smoke, embers, particles, all-caps type with wide tracking. The product half is bright: glossy white device renders on an infinite white floor with soft reflections, brand red as the only accent. Typography is a thin wide sans for the claims and a typewriter face for the tease.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- Real app screens mapped onto 3D phone and tablet models; no cursor, no finger, no taps.
- The camera does all the work: it orbits the device, pushes in to read one list (0:53), and slides at a grazing angle across the screen (1:07).
- Short benefit lines sit beside the device, one at a time (1:00).
- Screens are static: nothing is clicked, typed or navigated, so the device is shown, not used.

## Motion and transitions
The opening 13 s are a burst of smoke wipes (nine detected transitions), each line pushed out by drifting smoke. Then one long continuous camera move through the device section: orbit, push-in, slide, re-frame, with devices swapping by gliding past camera. Smooth, floaty ease; the devices move like products on a turntable.

## Pacing
Slow for its length. Voice is sparse (92 words/min), with big gaps: the first 35 s are a type tease with no product at all. The device section holds each screen for 3–4 s. The "YES!" beat is a one-word answer used as a pause before the logo.

## Sound
Moderate loudness (-16.9 LUFS). Male narration, slow and punchy, written as short statements with rhetorical questions near the end. Music is a building cinematic-pop bed (~118 BPM). SFX density (81 events/min) suggests whooshes on the smoke wipes and sparkle on the particles, not verified by ear.

## What to take from it
- 0:49–0:56: one continuous camera move orbits the phone and pushes in until a single list fills the frame; a device can be a camera path rather than a static mockup.
- 1:07: a grazing low angle across the screen gives depth to a flat UI.
- 1:18: a one-word answer alone on white as a beat of rest before the logo.

## Avoid
- Smoke wipes, ember particles and particle-written text: banned clichés (particles, light-leak-like flashes) and dated.
- A 35 s type-only tease before any product appears; the owner's hook must land by 2 s.
- Glossy old-generation phone renders, and an on-screen typo in a benefit line (1:00).
- Device renders where nothing is pressed; the cause of each change is never shown.

## Ideas for the motion-studio launch film
- Previs Studio tour: one unbroken camera path that orbits the browser window, pushes in to the timeline, then grazes low across the option pills, while a real cursor clicks them.
- Results chapter: a single-word answer (for example the final score) alone on the frame for one beat before the next section.
