## Visual style
- A strict two-tone system: pure white and black frames alternating, one hot-pink accent, one rounded geometric sans in two weights.
- Very large type (about 6–8 % of frame height), with the key word in bold pink.
- The sparkle logo is the only illustration. The UI pieces (input pill, chips, stat cards) are the graphics.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- **Real UI, isolated and blown up:** the input, reply, Sources card and stat card each fill the frame without app chrome, legible on a phone.
- **Typing with a caret the camera follows:** the pan keeps the caret near centre, so the sentence's start slides off the left edge (0:04, 0:13, 0:29).
- **Streaming reply:** the letters arrive in bursts with loose tracking that tightens as they settle (0:15). Reads as "thinking".
- **Chips fill in** as the question is understood (0:15, 0:32). **A loading state** shows before the answer (0:32).
- **One explicit click:** the cursor presses send (0:43).
- **The stat is the payoff:** each loop ends on a giant pink percentage.

## Motion and transitions
- Hard cuts between black and white frames act as punctuation (0:06, 0:53, 1:00).
- Horizontal whip-slides with heavy motion blur go from one UI piece to the next (0:18, 0:35).
- The caret-follow pan carries the typing beats, and the rotating sparkle is the recurring transition.
- Snappy fast-out with a soft land; no bounce on the type.

## Pacing
- Three full ask → answer loops in 48 s, about 3 s per idea.
- The "Spark your next ___" refrain returns five times and acts as the beat counter.
- It breathes on the black benefit cards.

## Sound

## What to take from it
- **0:01–0:04 a typed prompt with the camera following the caret:** exactly the feel for "talk to Claude Code".
- **0:15 a streaming reply while filter chips fill in:** shows an agent understanding a brief.
- **0:32 → 0:35 a loading spinner, then a giant stat sliding in with motion blur:** the wait and the payoff in 3 s.
- **0:43 the cursor presses send:** a visible cause before the answer.
- **0:26–0:52 a refrain with a word-slot swap:** a reusable structure that also paces the film.

## Avoid
- No VO. The owner wants dense narration, so Sarah would need to sit over this grammar.
- Type running past the frame edge mid-pan: check the 9:16 safe areas.
- Benefit cards on black that feel like title cards.
