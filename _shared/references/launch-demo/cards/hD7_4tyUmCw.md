# SaaS Explainer Video for Sales & Support Teams | JustCall | Dynamic Animation

- **Link:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hD7_4tyUmCw · **Channel:** What a Story - The Video Partner for SaaS & AI · **Length:** 1:10
- **Type:** ad / promo (kinetic type + stylised UI, music only) · **Product:** JustCall, cloud contact-centre software for sales and support teams
- **Numbers:** 2.6 transitions per min · median shot 17.2 s (7.8–45.1 s) · ~0 words/min (no VO) · ~99 BPM · 160.1 sound events/min · -18.9 LUFS

## Structure (beats with times)
- 0:00–0:10 **Hook:** a question in bold sans on a pale dotted ground, mint specks drifting (0:01); a hand-drawn question mark squiggles beside the answer line (0:04); a single mint dot travels across the empty frame (0:07).
- 0:10–0:17 **Brand:** the logo slides in while a giant mint circle sweeps in from the right (0:10); the circle wipes into stock footage of an agent on a headset with a two-line slogan (0:13–0:16).
- 0:17–0:28 **What it does:** CRM logos (HubSpot, Zendesk, Salesforce, G Suite) pop around her as circular badges (0:19); a wireframe globe with a calling headline (0:22) and a live call card sliding in from the right (0:25); on navy, a vertical word roll ticks through Take Calls → Send SMS → Set Sales Automation → Conversational Workflows, the active line bright, the others dim (0:17–0:28).
- 0:28–0:35 **Workflows:** a flow builder: one green campaign node branches to two outcome cards with drawn connectors (0:31); the panel exits right (0:34).
- 0:35–0:52 **Live calls and analytics:** headline (0:36), then a dashboard where a donut fills and two line charts draw left to right (0:39–0:42); a stats grid counts up (82 %, 32, 29, 4 s) (0:45); headline (0:48); live-calls panel with a big counter and a team list (0:51).
- 0:52–1:02 **Team management:** headline builds in two steps (0:54–0:57); team status table with a 60-available ring (1:00).
- 1:02–1:10 **CTA:** the mint dot grows into a speech-bubble blob that travels (1:02); logo with a slogan whose middle word swaps from Support to Business (1:05–1:08), Try-it-now pill.

## Visual style
Pale grey dotted ground, navy text and one mint accent; a single navy plate for the word roll. Medium-weight rounded sans, headlines in two lines, set left or right of centre, never centred-and-alone for long. UI is a clean redrawn version of the product: white cards, thin strokes, small brand mark top left.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- Redrawn, simplified dashboard panels that float frontal with a soft shadow.
- The data animates instead of a cursor: donut fills, lines draw, numbers count up (0:39–0:45); flow connectors draw from node to node (0:31).
- A call card slides in over the globe as the "action" (0:25). No cursor, no clicks, no typing anywhere.

## Motion and transitions
Only two hard cuts in 70 s. Everything else is carried by one mint element: a dot that drifts, becomes the circle wipe into footage (0:10), and returns as the speech-bubble blob (1:02). Headlines build word by word from small vertical offsets; panels slide out sideways to clear the frame. Smooth ease-out, unhurried.

## Pacing
Calm and spaced: each headline holds 3–5 s and each UI panel 5–8 s. With no voice, information comes only from short headlines, so it feels more like a brand ad than a demo.

## Sound
No speech. Light, upbeat electronic-pop bed (~99 BPM); 160 events/min suggests busy percussion plus small ticks on word entrances. Mixed quieter than most (-18.9 LUFS).

## What to take from it
- Vertical word roll: one bright active line with dimmed neighbours, ticking through capabilities (0:17–0:28).
- A single accent-coloured dot as the carry element across the whole film (0:07 → 0:10 → 1:02).
- Data animating as the proof: donut fills, lines draw, counters tick (0:39–0:45).
- Flow-builder connectors drawing from a node to its branches (0:31).
- End slogan with one swapped word (1:05–1:08).

## Avoid
- No voice and no cursor: it informs but never demonstrates, the exact gap in our draft.
- Stock headset footage.
- Wireframe globe for "global" is a cliché.
