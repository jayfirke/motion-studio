## Visual style
Clean flat 2D on near-white: blue-violet as the lead, with orange, teal and coral accents taken from the brand. Characters are simple geometric people with no outlines. UI is the real product (blue header, white module tiles with coloured icons). Type is minimal: logo lockups and UI text only, no headline slabs.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- Real UI screens as flat floating windows on white, no device frames.
- Module tiles pop in one at a time with a small scale overshoot, building the dashboard live (0:18–0:21).
- Illustration and UI share one canvas: characters sit next to windows, and the window shrinks so icons can orbit it (0:23).
- A design-tool style marquee selection (dashed box, corner handles) plus an arrow cursor frames a group of features as one "workspace" (0:41).
- Cursor clicks on the two CTA buttons (0:56, 0:59).

## Motion and transitions
Almost no cuts: the camera pans and scales across one long canvas, people slide in from the sides, icons burst out radially with staggered timing. The one cut (43.3 s) is a zoom-out reveal that the scene was inside the product all along. Easing is bouncy-ish on icons, smooth on camera.

## Pacing
Quick: a new element every 1–2 s, a new scene every 5 s, narration at 147 words/min. The product UI gets about 20 of the 61 seconds; the rest is characters. The only pause is the logo turn at 0:13–0:18.

## Sound
Male narrator, upbeat and smooth, problem-question opener then a list of features. Light corporate pop-electronic bed at about 123 BPM. Moderate SFX (90 events/min): pops on icons and tiles, whooshes on pans, clicks on the CTA buttons.

## What to take from it
- Dashboard building itself tile by tile as the voice names the modules (0:18–0:21).
- Marquee selection box with handles and a cursor grouping features, like a design tool (0:41).
- Zoom-out reveal that the previous scene was inside the app (0:43).
- Two cursor clicks to close: sub-brand, then main sign-up (0:56–0:59).

## Avoid
- Icon bursts around a stressed worker (0:03): a stock-explainer cliché.
- Tumbling 3D cube before the logo (0:13) is a spin.
- Bouncy overshoot on UI tiles; flat generic characters feel template-made.
