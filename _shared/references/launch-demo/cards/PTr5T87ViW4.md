## Visual style
- Bright, airy SaaS look: a soft periwinkle blur gradient with white glass cards.
- The brand's four-colour ring is broken into arcs and ribbons and used as the motion system.
- People are cut-out photos in flat green circles.
- Real third-party logos give instant context.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- Real-looking product screens with no device frame, floating on the gradient.
- An arrow cursor clicks "Insert" right after a short typing beat (0:41).
- Highlight sweeps mark the rows the AI picked (0:35).
- Integrations are shown by keeping the same panel and swapping the host app, with only a logo badge popping on the corner (0:44–0:51). That is three integrations in about 7 s.

## Motion and transitions

- **Everything morphs:** persona → arcs → logo → ribbon → nodes → UI.
- The logo-arc spin is used twice as a bookend (0:10, 1:06).
- The only hard cuts are the gag burst (0:51–0:54).
- Smooth ease-outs; springy pops on badges.

## What to take from it
- **Logo-as-motion-system (0:10–0:20):** the brand ring breaks into arcs and ribbons that carry the viewer from persona to features. One graphic idea links every section.
- **Type a few letters, then click Insert (0:41):** a micro-demo with visible cause and effect in under 2 s.
- **Same panel, different host (0:44–0:51):** swap the app around a constant panel and pop a logo badge. It shows "works everywhere" fast.
- **Highlight sweep on rows (0:35):** shows what the AI chose at a glance.

## Avoid
- The celebrity reaction-face meme gag. It risks image rights, dates quickly and is off-brand for us.
- Spinning arcs around a head run close to the "spins" cliché.
- The −20.9 LUFS master.

## Ideas for the motion-studio launch film
- **"Any agent":** keep one constant terminal panel and swap the host around it (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode), popping each logo badge on the corner, 1.5 s each.
- **"Talk to Claude Code":** type a few words of the prompt with a caret, then click send. The cause is visible in under 2 s.
