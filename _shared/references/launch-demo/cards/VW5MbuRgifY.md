## Visual style

The accent changes per scene (green for meters and tagline, red for the donut and chart, orange for the curve), so there is no single brand colour. The only 3D piece is the packshot.

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- This is a trailer for sounds, so the UI is the instrument: every sound has a visible cause. A toggle flips, a meter fills, a bar grows, a segmented control is clicked, an icon is hovered, a word is swapped (0:09, 0:16, 0:24, 0:27.6, 0:29).
- One object per beat, centred and large (toggle at 0:24, meters at 0:27.6), so the eye knows exactly where a sound belongs.
- Counting numbers and a donut give the pack a spec sheet without a table (0:12.5).
- Cursor: macOS-style arrow, used on the selector (0:10) and the dock (0:16).

## What to take from it
- Pair every sound with a clearly visible cause on a single centred object: toggle (0:24), meters (0:27.6), bars (0:29–0:31).
- A level meter that fills to a different height per bar (0:27.6): three sounds, three heights, one frame.
- Text that rewrites itself word by word instead of fading, which gives a natural slot for a tick or soft swish (0:04–0:06, 0:21–0:22.5).
- Segmented control with a cursor click on one option (0:09–0:11), a real UI moment that wants one real click.
- Specs as a counting number and a donut (0:12.5).

## Avoid
- Per-scene accent colours (green, red, orange): breaks the one-accent rule.
- The 0.08 s punch-in at 16.56 and the colour-shuffling letters at 0:02.5: close to glitch.
- 2.2 sounds per second: a product film should have fewer sounds than beats, and a sound that fires on every pixel of motion ends up sounding like a game.
- Any toggle or chart that ends in a "success" ding.

## Ideas for the motion-studio launch film
1. Sound palette check for chapters 5 and 6: for each planned effect, name the object that causes it on screen first (click on a button press, switch on a toggle, soft swell on a camera move), and drop any effect that has no visible cause.
2. In the Previs Studio tour, show the option selector as a segmented control (A / B / C) with a cursor click on one option, and give that click the one real mouse-click sample.
3. In "build and critique", a row of three level meters (voice, music, foley) filling to different heights while Sarah names them, with a tiny tick as each settles.
