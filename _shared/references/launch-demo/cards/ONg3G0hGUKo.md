## Visual style
Very light lavender-blue gradient ground, white rounded cards with soft shadows, blue-to-purple for AI (the sparkle, headings), green for positive, orange for negative and alerts. A thin geometric sans for the logo and headings, small UI type inside cards. UI is redrawn and plausible, not captured. A "Created by What a Story" badge sits top-left on most frames (agency bug, gone on the last two).

## How the product UI is shown (the demo grammar)
- A large label plus a card: the feature name in two bold lines on one side, the UI on the other (Themes & Sub-Themes Overview at 1:17, AI Summaries at 1:24), so the screen is named before it is read.
- Cause and effect as a chain with connector lines: bell, negative card, ticket, AI suggestion, message (1:31–1:38). Each card lands in turn.
- The product reaches a person's phone as a lock-screen notification (1:51.5), which proves the alert without a screenshot of a settings page.
- The copilot is a chat: question, a ranked answer, a typed follow-up, then recommendations (2:08–2:22). A cursor travels to the send button.
- A blue four-point sparkle marks every AI action (0:18, 1:09, 2:00).

## Motion and transitions
Mostly no cuts: 0:49–1:51 is one continuous 62-second move where cards slide, scale and replace each other while the ground stays. The two real shifts are the stock-photo problem to the soft ground (0:17) and the phone beat (1:51.5). Eases are smooth with a short settle, nothing bouncy.

## Pacing
Long and steady: about 13 feature beats in 2:56, roughly 13 s per beat, 118.5 wpm, with a tell-then-show rhythm. It is the film closest to the 3:50 length of ours in this batch, and it shows how far one voice can carry when the screen is constantly changing. The slow point is the middle (1:09–1:27) where three analytics screens run in a row.

## Sound
Calm, even corporate narration (inferred from the transcript, not heard) in complete sentences. The caption track puts [music] tags at sentence ends, so the bed swells on turns. The master is quiet (-19.4 LUFS) and sparse at 58 sound events per min, probably soft UI ticks on card landings.

## What to take from it
- The "close the loop" chain: a problem appears, a ticket is created, a fix is suggested and sent, one card at a time (1:31–1:38).
- The copilot chat with a typed follow-up, so the viewer sees the agent reason over data (2:08–2:22).
- A feature name set big beside the UI before the UI is read (1:17, 1:24).
- A 62-second unbroken UI tour with no hard cut (0:49–1:51).
- The notification landing on a phone as the proof of an alert (1:51.5).

## Avoid
- The stock-footage problem (0:03–0:16): our film has its own problems.
- The agency badge in the corner: a corner label.
- The three photo panels Predictive, Proactive, Personalized (2:37): a slogan strip with no job.
- The long run of analytics screens (1:09–1:27) with only a table to look at.

## Ideas for the motion-studio launch film
1. Build and critique: a chain of cards joined by connector lines, critique score fails, a fix list appears, a re-render starts, a pass lands, one card per beat.
2. Talk to Claude Code: a chat where the owner asks a question, gets a ranked answer, then types a follow-up and the screen reacts.
3. Hand-off: a notification card landing on a phone-sized frame, "Film approved, handed to the engine".
